Strikee, explained

Strikee is counter software for Indian snooker halls, sports courts, turfs and PS5 gaming cafés. Staff start a timer per table, bill by the frame or the hour, and every rupee lands in a daily cashbook. This page walks through a venue's night the way Strikee handles it, and notes what Tawn could do differently.

The short version

What it does well

  • Knows the domain: frames, slots, khata, "losers pay".
  • A settle screen that won't take payment until the amounts add up.
  • Ledger rows that name the staff member who made them and can only be changed where they came from.

Where it falls short

  • No till count or day close, and the cash totals disagree with the list below them.
  • No way to refund a payment once it's settled.
  • Nothing loads offline, and bookings can't be paid online.

Openings for Tawn

  • A real day close that matches the counted till.
  • Proper refunds and corrections, and online booking with payment.
  • A native counter app that keeps working without a connection.

Three ways to split the bill

Regular

One customer pays everything. They can keep several sessions open until they leave.

Losers pay

Pick the players. At the end staff mark who lost, and the cost becomes their debt.

Split pay

The cost splits equally. Each player gets their own debt for their share.

  • Prices are saved packages per table, such as "Per Frame ₹120" or "Per Hour ₹250". Each one also says whether money is taken upfront or at the end. Staff can override either for one session. Seen in app
  • Picking a sport sets the pricing model. Snooker bills per frame, badminton per 60-minute slot, football turf per 90 minutes. Seen in app
  • Frames are counted by hand. The session starts at 1 frame, and staff bump the count from the session card as play goes on. Seen in app
For Tawn

Bill modes and saved price packages are worth copying almost as they are. Hand-counted frames are a weak spot: a one-tap "next frame" on the table card, or a timer-based estimate, would cut counter errors.

Steps 4–5

Settling the tab

  • One payment can use several methods. Cash ₹70 plus UPI ₹50 for a ₹120 frame is normal, and the receipt shows both. Seen in app
  • Store credit is applied first, automatically. In the demo a ₹400 refund had become store credit, and ₹120 of it paid the next frame. Seen in app
  • Coupons are entered here and can be undone before payment. A deposit already paid is never touched. Seen in app
  • "Credit" is a payment method. Choosing it puts the amount on the customer's khata. Seen in app
  • The payment button stays disabled until the amounts balance. The screen says "Payment balanced" or "Over by ₹70". Seen in app
  • Each method becomes its own cashbook row, all under one invoice number. Seen in app
  • The Sessions list doesn't refresh afterwards. The tab still looks open until the page is reloaded. Bug in app
Step 6

The cashbook and the business day

The cashbook shows one business day at a time. That day runs from 5 AM to 5 AM, so a frame played at 1:30 AM still counts toward the evening it started in.

5 AM, 17 Nov 6 PM midnight 5 AM, 18 Nov

The blue dot is a payment at 1:30 AM on the 18th. It is counted in the 17 Nov business day.

  • Six totals at the top: today's balance, cash in hand, expenses, UPI/card, credit used and credit settled. Seen in app
  • Expenses have a "paid from sales till" switch. It separates money that left the till from money the owner paid personally. Seen in app
  • Rows that came from a session are read-only here. To change one, staff edit the session, so the books can't drift away from the floor. Manual rows can be edited or deleted. Seen in app
  • No till count or day close, on today or any past day. The website's headline promise is a cashbook that matches the till at closing. The app only shows running totals. Missing in app
  • The totals contradict the list. With ₹2,300 of cash and ₹650 of UPI income listed, "Cash in hand" and "UPI / Card" both read ₹0, and stayed at ₹0 after a new payment. Bug in app
  • Recurring expenses such as rent and salaries are marked "coming soon". Roadmap
For Tawn

This is the clearest gap. A close-the-day step (count the cash, compare with what was booked, record the difference, lock the day) is what owners are told they're buying, and Strikee doesn't have it. The totals have to be right before a day close means anything. Copy the 5 AM cutoff, the till switch and the read-only session rows.

Fixing mistakes and giving money back

The second review pass tried what a counter does when something goes wrong. This is where Strikee is weakest.

  • An open session can be moved to another customer, edited or deleted. Editing changes frames, price and times. Seen in app
  • Delete is owner-only and logged with the owner's name. But it called a session holding a ₹10 deposit "a clean delete", and its advice to "use Cancel instead" points to an action that doesn't exist. Bug in app
  • No refund for a settled payment. Receipts, paid tabs, cashbook rows and the member profile have no refund action. Refunds exist in the sample data (₹400 returned as store credit), so it may sit behind a role or state the demo can't reach. Missing in app
  • An overpaid tab can't be closed. When a session price was lowered below the deposit, closing the tab failed with a mismatch error. The only way out was to put the price back up. Bug in app
  • The till passcode wasn't set in the demo, so deletes and edits went through without a prompt. Seen in app
For Tawn

Treat corrections as first-class. Refunds should be reversing entries to cash, UPI or store credit. Deleting should be for sessions with no money attached, with a separate void for the rest. When a tab is overpaid, offer the difference back as change or store credit. All of it goes behind the owner's passcode.

Bookings

Staff can book from a 30-minute slot board covering today and tomorrow. Players can also ask for a booking from the venue's public page, but that's a request, not a booking:

New→ Awaiting payment→ Confirmed→ Closed

The dashed step is manual. Staff WhatsApp a UPI QR, wait for the advance, then confirm. Only then does the booking appear on the slot board.

For Tawn

Instant booking with payment at the point of request removes the manual middle step completely. Strikee chose not to process payments, so this is a real difference rather than a small feature.

The owner's side

Admin

Runs the floor: sessions, payments, members, credit limits and even pricing. Can't see revenue or export.

Co-owner

Adds the dashboard, financials, exports and team management.

Owner

The only one who can refund, delete sessions, transfer ownership or delete the venue.

  • Till lock: an optional 4-digit passcode guards refunds, deletions, credit settlement and pricing, even for staff who are logged in. It locks after 15 minutes idle. Seen in app
  • No floor-only role. The lowest role can still change prices. Seen in app
  • Reports for the accountant: P&L, member ledger, staff payout summary and a custom report, as PDF or CSV. Seen in app
  • Dashboard: overview, financial, customer and operations tabs, including peak hours, retention and plain-sentence insights and recommendations. It opens on the current month, which was empty in the demo. The data is under Yearly. Seen in app
For Tawn

Open the dashboard on a period that has data, such as the last 7 days. An empty first screen makes the product look broken.

When the connection drops

Strikee installs like an app, but it still needs the internet.

  • Pages don't load offline. Clicking a menu item or reloading shows the browser's "No internet" page. Tested in app
  • An open page keeps working, with no banner to say the connection is gone. Tested in app
  • Saves wait and retry. An expense saved offline sat on "Saving…" and went through once the connection returned. If the tab had been closed or reloaded, the entry would have been lost, and staff get no warning. Tested in app
  • Lists don't always keep up. The Sessions list can hang on "Loading active sessions…", and the sidebar's live count can disagree with it. Bug in app
For Tawn

Counter staff in small towns lose signal often. Store sessions and payments on the device, show clearly what hasn't synced yet, and send it when the connection returns, even if the app was closed in between.

Growth and pricing

  • Public venue pages at strikee.in/{venue} list prices by player count with peak and off-peak columns, consoles, games, photos and an FAQ written for Google. They double as Strikee's marketing, with "Page by Strikee" at the bottom. Seen live
  • Strikee runs three venues of its own in Navsari (two PS5 lounges and a pool and snooker hall), listed on strikee.in like any customer. The flagship lounge's page didn't load. Seen live
  • The tournament wizard does the arithmetic. It turns field size and group settings into "4 groups of 4 · 24 group matches · 7 knockout matches", and an entry fee and house cut into the prize pot. Seen in app
  • Tournament limits: entries go through WhatsApp or Instagram, only Snooker, EA FC 26 and FC 27 are supported, and the prize split doesn't rescale when the pot changes. Seen in app
  • Pricing scales with tables, not staff. Pro (₹449/mo) covers up to 4 units. Ultra (₹599/mo) has no limit and adds tournaments, the public page and memberships. Seen in app
  • The trial goes read-only instead of deleting. After 30 days new sessions stop and staff are logged out, but the data stays readable. Seen in app
  • The upgrade page uses the venue's own numbers, for example "₹16.44 a day" and "₹4.76 per logged session". Seen in app

What didn't add up

Some of these may come from the demo's sample data rather than the product.

The money problems (cashbook totals, refunds, overpaid tabs, the delete dialog) are covered above. The rest:

  • A member's history was empty."No game sessions or payments yet" for a customer who had paid twice that day.
  • A busy table shows as free.The session wizard offered a table as "Free now" while an overstayed session was still open on it.
  • Store credit shows as negative credit usage.A member holding ₹400 of store credit is shown as "-80%" of their credit limit.
  • Wrong labels.The edit dialog calls a snooker table's price "Console", and the venue switcher says "No tables yet" for a venue with 13.
  • Plan prices don't match.Settings shows "Professional ₹399/month". The public price list says Pro is ₹449.
  • Prize numbers don't match.One public tournament page lists a ₹2,500 prize pool and a ₹15,000 winner's prize.

Ideas to borrow

Useful whatever Tawn turns out to be.

  1. 5 AM business-day cutoffLate-night play counts toward the right evening.
  2. Saved price packages per tableEach has a unit, a duration and an upfront-or-later rule, and can be overridden per session.
  3. Group bill modesRegular, Losers pay and Split pay, each creating a debt per player.
  4. "Paid from sales till" switch on expensesKeeps till cash apart from the owner's own money.
  5. Refunds as store creditOffered automatically at the next payment.
  6. Passcode on risky actionsLighter than a full role system.
  7. Credit watchlist in bandsGrouped by share of the limit used, with a one-tap message.
  8. Two kinds of couponPrinted single-use codes that fail when photocopied, and shared codes with limits per phone number.
  9. A trial that ends read-onlyPlus an upgrade page priced in the venue's own numbers.
  10. Public venue and tournament pagesThey work as search pages, a booking inbox and free acquisition, with no payment gateway needed.
  11. A settle screen that can't go wrongStore credit applied by default, pick which sessions to settle, split across methods, and no submitting until it balances.
  12. Ledger rows owned by their sourceCashbook rows from a session change only through the session, so the books match the floor.
  13. A tournament wizard that does the arithmeticGroups, match counts and the prize pot are worked out as you go.
  14. Prices by player count, peak and off-peakFor consoles shared by up to four players.
